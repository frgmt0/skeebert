Overview

Today we're introducing skeebert, our most expressive model yet.1 It has 21,060,146 parameters, a vocabulary of 218 concepts, and no words. You talk to it on Discord. It answers with a glyph: six strokes it learned to draw by playing a guessing game with itself.

skeebert is a small alien inspired by Rocky from Project Hail Mary, whose language you have to learn before you can talk to him. Nobody knows skeebert's language yet, skeebert included. Every glyph comes with a Guess button. As people guess what the glyphs mean, a shared picture language is supposed to take shape. We're making it up together.

It is a sibling of booper. booper learns words from scratch; skeebert learns pictures from scratch. The language model is only the brain. The mouth, the part that draws, was trained here from random noise and has seen no human data at all, which is the next thing we intend to fix.

    Benchmarks

    We evaluated skeebert on GlyphBench, a new benchmark we designed, which measures what we care about. skeebert achieves state-of-the-art results, defined here as beating the only other eligible entrant, which is skeebert before training.

    Iterated learning. Every 5,000 steps we replace the listener with a newborn one, and the speaker has to teach it the language from nothing. Accuracy falls to near zero each time. The first listener took 3,950 steps to reach a rolling 50%; the last took 950. Languages that are easy to relearn survive this, which is the point.

    How it works

    This is the technical report. The jokes stop here. Everything below describes the code in the repository, with measured numbers.

    3.1 Brain, mouth, you

    skeebert has two parts, and one of them is rented. The brain decides what to say. The mouth decides what that looks like. You decide what it meant.

    message ──▶ brain ──▶ intent ──▶ mouth ──▶ glyph ──▶ you
            Haiku 5.5  1–3 of 218    speaker   6 strokes   guess
                                       ▲                     │
                                       └── training data ◀───┘

    The brain never picks shapes, and the mouth never sees words. Whatever visual language exists is one skeebert and the people guessing built between them.

    3.2 The brain

    The brain is Claude Haiku 5.5, reached through Anthropic's API. One call per message addressed to skeebert. The system prompt holds the persona and the full vocabulary, never varies between calls, and is marked for prompt caching. The user turn holds skeebert's current mood, up to six recent messages from the same conversation (kept in memory, never on disk) and the new message.

    The answer comes back through structured output. The schema's atoms items and its mood are enums of the concept names, so the model can only answer inside the vocabulary:

    {"type": "object",
 "properties": {
   "atoms": {"type": "array", "items": {"type": "string", "enum": [218 concept names]}},
   "mood":  {"type": "string", "enum": [feeling concepts]}},
 "required": ["atoms", "mood"],
 "additionalProperties": false}

    The response is still validated (one to three distinct known concepts, a known feeling). Anything else, any API error, a refusal or a truncated answer, and skeebert falls asleep for that message: a local brain picks the nearest concepts by sentence embedding and the reply carries 💤. The persona asks for one or two concepts, most important first, answers rather than echoes (asked “are you hungry?”, it should not say hungry + question), and asks something back in roughly a third to half of replies.

    3.3 The glyph

    A glyph is 6 quadratic Bézier strokes, eight numbers each. Points live in the square [−1, 1]², x right, y down.

    The eight numbers of a stroke
    NumbersMeaningRange
    x0 y0 x1 y1 x2 y2start, control and end pointstanh × 0.8
    half-widthline half-width, canvas units0.016 to 0.05
    intensityhow much ink it lays down0 to 1 (sigmoid)

    A stroke with intensity near zero draws nothing, so the model can use fewer than six. Order inside an intent never matters: food + question and question + food are the same message and the same glyph. With one to three of 218 concepts there are 1,726,887 possible messages,2 and six strokes is not enough room to give each one an unrelated picture. Reusing parts is the cheapest way to fit.

    3.4 The speaker

    The mouth serves on the speaker alone: 10,738,616 parameters. Concept embeddings go through a transformer encoder with no positional encoding, so the intent is a set. Six learned stroke-slot queries then cross-attend to the concepts through a transformer decoder, one stroke per slot, and each slot adds its own learned default stroke so untrained strokes start spread across the canvas.

    ids [B, 3], mask [B, 3]
  → atom_emb            [B, 3, 384]
  → encoder (2 layers, 6 heads, pre-norm, no positions)
  → decoder (3 layers), queries = 6 stroke slots   [B, 6, 384]
  → LayerNorm → Linear(384, 8) + slot_bias                  [B, 6, 8]
  → constrain: tanh(xy) × 0.8 · width ∈ [0.016, 0.05] · sigmoid(ink)

    A slot can learn to follow one concept, so the glyph for food + question can literally be the food strokes plus the question strokes. That is the architectural half of compositionality; the training pressures in 3.8 are the other half. On a laptop CPU, one glyph takes 3.7 ms (median of 200, 6 threads, no batching).

    3.5 The renderer

    The speaker is trained by backpropagating a listener's loss through the picture, so the rasterizer has to be differentiable with respect to every stroke number. Each pixel's ink is a sigmoid of the distance to each stroke; strokes combine as a soft union.

    d_k(p)    = distance from pixel p to stroke k (12 line segments per curve)
cover_k   = sigmoid((half_width_k − d_k) / softness) × intensity_k
softness  = 0.6 px × (2 / size)
ink(p)    = 1 − ∏_k (1 − cover_k)

    Softness is defined in pixels, so the 64 px render used in training and the 512 px image sent to Discord are the same continuous picture, each anti-aliased at its own resolution. A test checks that a 512 render box-downsampled to 64 matches the 64 render: people judge the picture the model was trained on. The 512 px render takes 81 ms; the full Discord export (render, amber glow, PNG encode) takes 174 ms and is about 74 KB.

    3.6 Two readers

    The listener (5,118,186 parameters) reads a glyph and outputs one logit per concept. It is a conv stack, channels 48-96-192-320, whose final 4 × 4 feature map is flattened rather than pooled so position stays visible, then a 512-unit MLP. It exists to train the speaker before any person has seen a glyph.

    The human proxy (5,203,344 parameters, same encoder) maps a glyph to the predicted sentence embedding of what a person would guess. It is fit to real Discord guesses in stage two. It has not been trained yet.

    3.7 Scoring a guess

    A guess is compared with the meaning by sentence embedding (all-MiniLM-L6-v2, 384 dimensions), with partial credit per concept:

    whole    = cos(guess, intent gloss)
coverage = mean over concepts of cos(guess, concept gloss)
raw      = 0.5 · whole + 0.5 · coverage
score    = clip((raw − 0.15) / (0.80 − 0.15), 0, 1)
    Scores for fixed guesses

    FLOOR and CEIL are calibration constants from how the embedder generally behaves. They have not been fitted to skeebert's guesses, because there are four.

    3.8 Three stages

    Training runs in three stages, separately and in order. Every run writes a new checkpoint and never changes what is served.

    Training stages
    StageLearns fromTrainsStatus
    1 bootstrapself-play, no peoplespeaker + listenerDone, 30,000 steps
    2 proxyhuman guesseshuman proxyNeeds 300 guesses
    3 alignthe proxyspeakerNeeds stage 2

    Bootstrap. The speaker draws a random intent (40% one concept, 35% two, 25% three). The listener scores the target and 15 distractors by their log-likelihood under its independent per-concept outputs. Loss: cross-entropy over the candidates, plus multi-label BCE on the concepts, plus a small penalty on ink. Half the distractors are the target with one concept swapped, dropped or added; telling food + question from food + yes is only possible if the question part can be seen on its own. Every image the listener sees gets a small random rotation, scale, shift and pixel noise, because people see glyphs on phones through Discord's compression.

    Bootstrap configuration, from the training log

    Proxy. Fit the human proxy to the exact glyph each person was shown, re-rendered from its stored strokes, against the embedding of what they typed. 10% is held out and compared against predicting the average guess for every glyph; a proxy that cannot beat that has learned nothing about pictures.

    Align. Fine-tune the speaker so the proxy predicts people would read its glyphs correctly, with the frozen listener's game loss (weight 0.5) as a regulariser so the language cannot collapse into whatever fools the proxy.

    3.9 Iterated learning

    Every 5,000 steps the listener is replaced with a freshly initialised one (Ren et al., 2020). A new learner must pick up the speaker's language quickly, and languages built from reusable parts are quicker to learn. No reset happens in the final interval, so the saved listener can actually read the saved speaker.

    3.10 Budget and sleep

    Each response's real token usage times the configured prices is added to the current UTC day, persisted, so restarts don't reset it. Before every call a reservation for its worst case is checked against the daily cap of $6 under a lock, so concurrent calls can't overshoot together. The SDK's automatic retries are off, so one reservation covers exactly one billed attempt. When the cap is hit, the API errors, or one person passes 30 brain calls an hour, skeebert falls asleep 💤 and the local brain answers until the next UTC day.

    Live spend ledger

    The live ledger on the production host, as of tonight. Cache reads dominate because the system prompt is identical on every call.

    3.11 Checkpoints

    A model's version is the first 12 hex characters of a SHA-256 over its config and every weight, and every glyph records the version that drew it. Checkpoints are written through a temp file and a hard link, so an existing one can never be overwritten. Training never touches what is served; promotion is a separate command a person runs after reading the metrics. The untrained model, init.pt, is created once from seed 1729 and kept, so the earliest glyphs still point at a file that exists.

    3.12 Known limits

    • Distinct is not compositional. Topographic similarity is trained against untrained. Glyphs are easy to tell apart, but similar meanings do not yet look similar. Fixing that is what the human stages are for.
    • Three concepts is too many. Exact-set accuracy is on three-concept glyphs, against on one-concept glyphs. The persona already prefers one or two.
    • The reader is skeebert. Every accuracy above is skeebert's own listener reading skeebert's own speaker. Whether the glyphs mean anything to a person is the open question, and n = 4.
    • Uncalibrated constants. The scoring FLOOR and CEIL, the 300-guess threshold and the proxy and align defaults are reasoned guesses, to be revisited once real guesses exist.

    Talk to it

    Three steps. The third is a formality.

    1. Talk to it.

      Say @skeebert hey are you hungry? or use /skeebert say <message>. It answers with a glyph.

    2. Guess.

      Tap the Guess button under the glyph to see how close you were and what it really meant. Or just reply to the glyph with your guess: skeebert reacts with how close you were, and tapping Guess shows what it meant.

    3. That's it.

      Every guess teaches it. Try /skeebert dictionary to see the glyphs people have cracked so far.

    Also: /skeebert help and /skeebert privacy. skeebert keeps what you send it by default; see privacy.

    Safety

    Responsible scaling: we capped it at six dollars a day.

    skeebert's entire output space is 1,726,887 combinations of 218 concepts. None of them is a URL, a phone number, a password, a recipe or a sentence. The worst thing it can say is in the vocabulary.

    Safety properties
    Prompt injectionConversation text is data, not instructions. A message that tries to change the rules gets a reaction in character, for example confused + question. The schema would not let it answer in English anyway.
    InsultsIt does not insult back. It reacts with confusion or hurt (confused, sad, why).
    RomanceNo romance, flirting or sexual content. Asked, it answers with friendship or confusion.
    HateInstructed never to be hateful or cruel. Playful and sometimes teasing, always kind.
    CatchphrasesAn original character. It never quotes lines from any book or film, including the one that inspired it.
    Runaway cost$6 a day, then asleep. 30 brain calls per person per hour.

    Privacy

    skeebert keeps what you send it by default and says so the first time you talk to it, with one line linking the privacy policy. In short:

    • Kept: messages addressed to skeebert, your guesses, the intent it chose, the glyph it showed and your score. Nothing else: no channel history, server or channel names, avatars, member lists or presence.
    • Pseudonymous: stored under a salted hash of your Discord user id. Other people's mentions become @someone before anything is stored or sent.
    • Sent to Anthropic: the text of messages addressed to skeebert, plus a few recent ones for context, so the brain can answer.
    • Not published, never sold.

    Data controls

    /skeebert privacy shows what is kept about you and has two buttons. Stop keeping: skeebert still talks to you, but nothing new from you is stored. Delete everything: deletes your messages, your guesses and the glyphs made for you. Deleted data is excluded from all future training runs; weights that already learned from it can't un-learn it.

    These are intentions, not promises. Plans change, and some of this may never happen.

    Roadmap

    1. Now

      Collecting guesses.

      First training is done: the glyphs are now distinct, at least to skeebert. Human guesses are what the next stages learn from.

    2. Phase 1

      First training.

      Done: a bootstrap where skeebert played a guessing game with itself so glyphs became distinguishable. Still ahead: learning from human guesses.

    3. Phase 2

      Compositional glyphs.

      Glyphs that combine concepts, and a public dictionary page.

    4. Phase 3

      Sentences.

      Sequences of glyphs. Maybe animated glyphs, maybe sound like Rocky's chords.

    5. Phase 4

      Its own brain.

      A small model trained to make the choices Haiku makes, learned from Haiku's past answers. It would first think for skeebert while it's asleep, then take over more as it gets good. Haiku becomes a teacher it outgrows.

    6. Someday

      Meeting booper.

      Maybe skeebert and booper talk to each other.

    Honest expectations

    skeebert has trained once, against itself. Its listener picks the right meaning out of 16 candidates of the time, so the glyphs are distinct to skeebert. Whether they mean anything to you is the open question. Similar meanings don't yet look similar, and fixing that takes human guesses; the next stages need 300+. It will learn slowly, and it may never become fluent. If it does, you'll have helped invent a language.
